Insights

Thinking we are willing to put in writing.

Perspectives on healthcare architecture, interoperability, identity, care models and governance, drawn from more than a decade of work across provider, payer, program and product environments. No vendor pitches, and no claims we would not defend in a design review.

Principles

Start with the problem, not the technology

The most expensive decisions in healthcare IT are made before the first requirement is written.

Read →Architecture

Enterprise-first, not project-first

Point solutions are individually rational and collectively ruinous. Enterprise architecture is how an organization stops paying for the same integration five times.

Read →Interoperability

Transport trust is not actor trust

Certificates prove who is on the wire. They do not prove who is allowed to ask, or why. What trusted exchange frameworks still leave to governance.

Read →Identity & consent

Delegated access is a design problem

Caregivers are not patients with someone else's login. Designing for them means designing identity, consent and audit as first-class features.

Read →Interoperability

Interoperability deadlines are architecture deadlines

Compliance projects that are run as compliance projects produce a checkbox. Run as architecture, they produce a data-exchange foundation the organization will use for a decade.

Read →Care models

Payment models are requirements

Care-management platforms fail when they are designed from the clinical workflow forward and the billing rules backward. Design from both at once.

Read →Governance

Measure governance by review burden, not technical difficulty

Architecture review boards fail in two ways: they become bottlenecks, or they become rubber stamps. The fix is to classify work by how much deliberation a decision needs.

Read →Modernization

Lift-and-shift is a decision, not a default

Cloud migration advice tends to come in two flavors: always refactor, or just move it. Both are wrong often enough that the choice deserves a real analysis.

Themes

What runs through all of it.

Problem before technology

Write the problem down with a measurement. Judge everything against it.

Enterprise before project

Every project is evaluated for what it leaves behind for the next one.

Standards before custom

Open standards outlive projects, vendors and staff. Custom needs a documented reason.

Rules are requirements

Regulations and payment models are sources of architecture, not obstacles to it.

Trust is designed, not asserted

Identity, consent and audit are features. Attestation is where governance begins, not ends.

Decisions, written down

An organization that can read its own architecture does not depend on any one person, including us.
