Architecture as a Service.

Strategic CIO and CTO leadership that thinks enterprise-first, not project-first, without the C-suite overhead. We give healthcare organizations access to senior architecture leadership on a fractional basis: enough to change the trajectory of the technology portfolio, scaled to what a community hospital, a growing practice group, a program office or a startup can actually afford.

The idea

Enterprise-first, not project-first.

Most healthcare technology is bought one project at a time. The result is an enterprise nobody designed.

Every project makes locally sensible decisions: this vendor, this interface, this workaround. Ten years later the organization is running dozens of systems that each made sense on their own and together make nothing easy. Interoperability becomes a fire drill, modernization becomes impossible to sequence and every new program starts with an integration project.

Architecture as a Service puts a single, enterprise-level point of view across all of those decisions. Not to slow them down, but to make sure each one leaves the organization better positioned for the next. It is the perspective a strong CIO or CTO would bring, offered as a service rather than a full-time hire, with the healthcare and standards depth that generalist IT leadership rarely has.

How we work

The working principles behind every engagement.

  • Problem first

    We will not recommend a technology until we can state the operational or financial problem it solves and how we will know it worked. If the problem turns out not to need technology, we say so.

  • Right-sized

    Enterprise architecture scaled to the organization. A rural hospital, a program office and a national plan need the same discipline at very different weights. We do not sell a 400-bed solution to a 25-bed hospital.

  • Standards over custom

    We design on open standards and implementation guides wherever they exist, because they outlive projects, vendors and staff. Custom is a last resort with a documented reason.

  • Decisions, written down

    Every engagement leaves behind decision records, diagrams and roadmaps that a future team can read. The goal is an organization that understands its own architecture, not one that depends on us.

  • Honest about uncertainty

    Regulations change, program details get clarified and vendors miss dates. We distinguish clearly between what is verified, what is likely and what still needs confirmation, and we flag it when a decision depends on something that has not been confirmed.

  • Phased and absorbable

    Change is delivered in phases the organization can absorb without disrupting care. We stay engaged through implementation rather than handing over a deck and leaving.
