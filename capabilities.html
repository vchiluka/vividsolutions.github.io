Capabilities

Technical depth where healthcare is hardest.

Our capabilities sit at the intersection of three things that rarely live in the same team: healthcare domain knowledge, standards-level interoperability expertise and disciplined enterprise architecture. Each capability below is something we have designed, built, reviewed or governed in production healthcare environments.

01

Interoperability & health data exchange

Standards are not a checkbox. They are the design language that lets a system outlive the project that funded it.

  • FHIR & USCDI implementation

    FHIR R4 resource modeling, profiling against US Core and USCDI data classes, implementation-guide conformance, and the operational realities of FHIR servers, terminology services and bulk data export. We design APIs that other organizations can actually consume.

    FHIR R4US CoreUSCDIBulk FHIRSMART on FHIR

  • Legacy integration standards

    HL7 v2 messaging, C-CDA documents, X12 EDI transactions, DICOM and imaging workflows, and the integration engines that carry them. Most enterprises run on these for years to come; we design the bridges between them and modern APIs rather than pretending they will disappear.

    HL7 v2C-CDAX12 EDIDICOM / RIS / PACS

  • Trusted exchange & network participation

    Readiness assessment and onboarding architecture for nationwide trusted exchange, health information networks and state HIEs, including exchange-purpose governance, participant and sub-participant obligations, and the audit and monitoring controls that make trust real rather than asserted.

    TEFCAQHIN onboardingHIEExchange purposes

  • Payer interoperability & prior authorization APIs

    Compliance architecture for the federal interoperability and prior authorization rules: Patient Access, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer and Prior Authorization APIs, with the data-mapping, consent, attribution and operational metrics those programs require.

    CMS-0057-FDa VinciCARINPatient Access API

  • Patient- and caregiver-mediated exchange

    Consumer-facing data access through claims and clinical APIs, verifiable health summaries using SMART Health Cards and Links, and application-ecosystem participation for the people and caregivers who need the data most.

    SMART Health LinksBlue Button 2.0App ecosystems
02

Identity, consent & delegated access

The hardest interoperability question is not how to move the data. It is who is allowed to see it, on whose behalf and with what proof.

  • Identity assurance

    Architecture for identity proofing and authentication at the assurance levels federal programs now expect, including integration patterns for credential service providers and the handling of identity tokens inside clinical and consumer applications.

    NIST 800-63IAL2 / AAL2OpenID Connect

  • Consent & proxy relationships

    Modeling consent, personal representatives and caregiver relationships as first-class data, with the distinctions that matter legally and clinically: delegating access, sharing a record, and acting as a representative are different things and should be designed differently.

    FHIR ConsentRelatedPersonHIPAA

  • Audit-everything design

    Disclosure logging as a product feature rather than a compliance afterthought. Every access and release recorded in a standard, queryable form so that privacy officers, auditors and the people whose data it is can all get the same answer.

    FHIR AuditEventAccounting of disclosures

Cloud, infrastructure & modernization

Right-sized, cloud-first and sequenced so the organization keeps operating while it changes.

  • Cloud migration strategy

    Migration planning for clinical, administrative and research workloads, including the honest trade-off between lift-and-shift and refactor, dependency and licensing analysis for legacy platforms, and target-state designs in regulated cloud environments.

    Lift-and-shift vs. refactorFedRAMP-awareHybrid

  • Legacy modernization & tech-debt reduction

    Application portfolio assessment, technical-debt inventory and a prioritized remediation sequence tied to business value and risk, so modernization dollars go where they change outcomes.

    Portfolio rationalizationDebt inventory

  • API, microservices & platform architecture

    API-first platform design with a stable core and well-defined extension points, so the same platform can serve multiple programs and populations without forking. Plugin-style data-source layers that let new sources drop in without core changes.

    Core + extension packsPlugin registryAPI gateway

  • Infrastructure governance

    Architecture review documentation, migration governance for identity and directory services, and the decision records that let a future team understand why the environment looks the way it does.

    ARB / TRB artifactsDecision records
